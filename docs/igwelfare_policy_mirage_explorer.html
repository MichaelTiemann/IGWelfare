1. Purpose: Connecting 4-Year Cash Budgets to 42-Year Intergenerational Welfare

Daily fiscal debate in Aotearoa New Zealand scores policy packages almost exclusively inside a 4-year operating cash window (OBEGALx in BEFU/PREFU), whereas demographic, health, infrastructure, and human-capital dynamics unfold across a 40-to-60-year generational horizon (the New Zealand Treasury's He Tirohanga Mokopuna Long-Term Fiscal Model, LTFM, spanning 2023–2065).

This explorer evaluates any combination of 18 ongoing institutional commitments (C01–C18) simultaneously as:

  • Short-Term Fiscal Mirages vs. Trim-Tab Seeds: How retrenching early-years, clinical, R&D, civic, or Smokefree commitments books small positive 4-year cash reliefs (+NZ$7.69B across the 12 public cuts) at the cost of compounding multi-decade welfare and solvency sinkholes (-NZ$186.77B in general equilibrium)—and conversely, how modest upfront 4-year seed outlays unlock supermodular long-run dividends when flipped to +Reform.
  • Siloed Partial Equilibrium vs. Coupled General Equilibrium: How evaluating policies one ministry at a time misses cross-portfolio feedbacks across housing rents, educator purchasing power, skilled youth migration, hospital congestion, and sovereign debt-service curvature.

2. Convention-2 Institutional Commitment Algebra

Every policy configuration is represented by a signed institutional commitment vector \(\mathbf{u} = (u_1, \dots, u_{18})^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{18}\) anchored to the pre-shock 2023 economy (SS0_2023 at \(\mathbf{u} = \mathbf{0}\)):

  • Public Capability, Tenure-Neutrality, Civic & Exergy Commitments (C01–C08, C12–C17): Oriented so \(u_i > 0\) strengthens institutional capacity or tax neutrality (+Reform) and \(u_i < 0\) enacts the calibrated 2024–2026 retrenchment, deferral, privatization, or leveraged-landlord tax restoration (Shock).
  • Tax-Relief, Investor-Visa, Asset-Expensing & Defence Levers (C09–C11, C18): Oriented so \(u_i > 0\) enacts the 2024–2026 statutory tax cut, visa inflow, immediate expensing, or unfunded 3.0%-of-GDP defence uplift, while \(u_i < 0\) tightens or offsets that schedule.
Additive Vector Composition & Pass-Through Scaling: Composing any two policy bundles \(A\) and \(B\) sums their underlying commitment vectors \(\mathbf{u} = \mathbf{u}_A + \mathbf{u}_B\) before resolving non-linear general-equilibrium couplings in a single pass: \[ u_i^{\text{eff}} = \begin{cases} s_{\text{core13}} \, u_i & \text{for } i \in \{1,\dots,13\} \quad (s_{\text{core13}} = 0.5915 \text{ macro announcement pass-through}), \\[4pt] s_{\text{direct}} \, u_i & \text{for } i \in \{14,\dots,18\} \quad (s_{\text{direct}} = 1.0000 \text{ direct statutory / balance-sheet wedges}). \end{cases} \]

2B. Five Asymmetric Reform Mirror Regimes: Capacity Bounds, Stern-Kicks & Behavioral Kinks

Liquidating or deferring an existing public stock does not share a linear symmetric functional form with expanding capacity or raising statutory rates above the pre-shock 2023 baseline (SS0_2023). Every commitment's +Reform mirror is governed by an explicit Asymmetric Mirror Scale \(m_i^{\text{pos}}\), a 4-year seed/fiscal scale \(C_i^{\text{pos}}\), and an onset gestation constant \(\tau_i^{\text{pos}}\) across five empirically grounded regimes:

  1. Regime 1 — Bounded Ratio & Sovereign Procurement Stern-Kicks (C18, C10):
    • C18 Sovereign Defence (+Reform Stern-Kick): Because baseline NZDF spending is 1.2% of GDP (~NZ$5.4B/yr), the +NZ$8.0B/yr shock to 3.0% of GDP cannot be mirrored by cutting NZ$8.0B/yr. Instead, +Reform holds the 1.2% baseline and invests a NZ$1.20B 4-year seed to shift procurement onshore into dual-use maritime/autonomous engineering (\(+z_{h,k}\)) paired with a defence-sector clean-energy transition (cutting imported fuel exposure \(\bar{e}_k\) and sovereign FX spread)—creating a +0.25 pp upfront debt stern-kick that crosses permanently below LTFM in 2037 (+NZ$11.18B PV dividend).
    • C10 Investor Visa (0.25x Flow Screen): Screens out passive luxury-land bidding while retaining active venture inflows.
  2. Regime 2 — Lumpy "Already Future-Proofed" Capital Projects (C04, C05 at 0.35x): Because SS0_2023 already delivered the future-proofed Dunedin Hospital build and rail-enabled Cook Strait ferries, +Reform adds bounded surgical-waitlist/staffing buffers (C05, NZ$0.30B) and electrified port shore-power/coastal trunk links (C04, NZ$0.45B, with zero fake "reverse break-fee" windfall).
  3. Regime 3 — Physical-Plant Stern-Kicks & Absorptive Ceilings (C02, C03, C12, C14, C17):
    • C02 Full R&D & Lab-Plant Expansion (1.69x Un-Diluted Aperture, \(\tau_{\text{pos}} = 12\text{ yrs}\)): Winding down Callaghan Innovation scatters intact teams overnight (\(\tau = 7.5\text{ yrs}\)), whereas full-scale CRI, Marsden, and deep-tech expansion requires building physical cleanrooms, pilot plants, and PhD pipelines first—modeled as a NZ$1.80B 4-year capital + R&D seed with a 12.0-year payoff lag (+0.44 pp stern-kick, crossing below LTFM in 2042 and reaching +NZ$15.17B PV welfare).
    • Workforce & Governance Caps: C03 Public Service (0.30x targeted analytical/IDI capability), C12 Kaiako Parity (0.50x retention ceiling), C14 Water Balance Sheet (0.55x spread saving), and C17 Grid Governance (0.45x Ostrom commercial-board turnaround).
  4. Regime 4 — High-Elasticity Early-Childhood, Tenure-Neutrality & Flagship Programs (C01, C06, C15, C16 at 0.80x–1.00x): Owner-occupier tax neutrality / speculative land holding charges (C01, 1.00x), Cunha-Heckman First-2000-Days wraparound ECE (C06, 0.80x), Document 1E's Universal Joiner Passport (C15, NZ$1.32B/4yr), and CERF household/grid electrification (C16, NZ$1.80B/4yr seed) move households and capital smoothly along high-return interior margins.
  5. Regime 5 — The 1/3-to-1/2 Behavioral Elasticity, Biological Hazard & Tax-Incidence Kink (C08, C07, C09, C11, C13 at 0.38x–0.50x): Where public-economics and epidemiological evidence establish a sharp kink at the 2023 baseline, +Reform is restricted to the empirically defensible 1/3-to-1/2 local neighborhood:
    • C08 Tobacco & Smokefree (0.38x Kink, \(\tau_{\text{pos}} = 10.5\text{ yrs}\)): Repealing Smokefree 2025 generational endgame rules (Shock, -NZ$4.49B) re-exposes non-addicted youth cohorts where initiation price/access elasticity is high (~-0.7) and avoids 40 years of cumulative pack-year exposure. Conversely, hiking tobacco taxes further above New Zealand's already world-leading 2023 excise (~NZ$1.60/cigarette) hits hardened adult nicotine dependence (Becker-Murphy / Gruber-Kőszegi residual elasticity ~-0.15 to -0.25), 25%–35% illicit-market leakage, regressive low-income budget crowd-out, and Doll-Peto biological irreversibility of prior pack-years. Modeling +Reform as a 0.38x targeted cessation, pharmacotherapy (NRT), and border-enforcement package yields a defensible +NZ$1.44B PV gain (~32% of the repeal harm rather than a spurious 100% mirror).
    • C09 Personal PAYE & C11 Corporate Base (0.45x / 0.40x Harberger-Chetty Kink): Unfunded tax cuts lose 100% of mechanical revenue into the sovereign debt snowball, whereas raising marginal rates above baseline faces convex Harberger deadweight loss (\(\text{DWL} \propto \tau^2 \cdot \text{ETI}\), Saez-Slemrod-Giertz) and trans-Tasman skill-migration sensitivity—confining revenue trims to the local Chetty (2012) optimization-friction band.
    • C13 KiwiSaver Match (0.45x Chetty Crowd-Out Kink) & C07 Tertiary Subsidies (0.50x Dynarski Infra-Marginal Kink): Halving KiwiSaver matches or repealing Fees-Free binds sharply on liquidity-constrained youth and 30%-IRR social tenants, whereas universal expansions above baseline encounter 65%–80% passive savings reshuffling among higher-income savers (Chetty et al. 2014, QJE) and infra-marginal tuition windfalls among non-constrained students (Dynarski 2003).

3. Set-Algebraic Subset Grammar, Logical Groupings & Dynamic Pruning

Any user selection or expression string (such as c01, c02, c02 or ret10, +c06, +c15, +c16) is normalized into a canonical ordered set \(S \subseteq \{1,\dots,18\}\) paired with a sign map \(\sigma: S \to \{-1, +1\}\). Duplicate tokens collapse idempotently (\(A \cup A = A\)).

Commitments partition into the four Document 3 portfolio lanes:

  • Lane 1 (Children, Health & Education): C05, C06, C07, C08, C12
  • Lane 2 (Housing, KiwiSaver, Water & Civic Commons): C01, C13, C14, C15, C17
  • Lane 3 (Science, State Capacity, Freight & Clean Exergy): C02, C03, C04, C10, C11, C16
  • Lane 4 (Working-Age Tax & Defence Fiscal Base): C09, C18

For any active set \(S\), let \(S_k = S \cap L_k\) and let \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{act}}(S) = \{k \in \{1,2,3,4\} : |S_k| \ge 1\}\) with \(m = |\mathcal{L}_{\text{act}}(S)|\) active lanes:

  • Logical Sub-Groupings (View A): A lane sub-group bar is generated if and only if \(2 \le |S_k| < |S|\) (singleton lanes are never duplicated as redundant group bars). When \(S = \{1,\dots,16\}\) in the 2024 shock direction, the grouping rules reproduce the exact Retrenchment + Landlord 10, Full 15 Domestic, and Full 16 Vector bars.
  • Dynamic Wedge Pruning (View B): Empty lanes (\(S_k = \emptyset\)) are pruned automatically. Wedge 1: Intra-Lane is included if and only if \(\max_{k} |S_k| \ge 2\) (for instance, when C05 hospital deferrals and C08 Smokefree repeal co-occur inside Lane 1). Cross-lane wedges are constructed strictly across the \(m\) active lanes \(k_1 < k_2 < \dots < k_m\).

4. Sequential Additionality Wedges vs. Order-Invariant Shapley Attribution

Let \(V(A)\) denote the Present-Value social welfare valuation of any subset of commitments \(A \subseteq S\). For the 2024–2026 Full-16 vector (\(S = \{1,\dots,16\}\)), the siloed partial-equilibrium sum is \(\sum_{i=1}^{16} V(\{i\}) = -\text{NZ\$225.61B}\), whereas the coupled general-equilibrium valuation is \(V(S) = -\text{NZ\$294.67B}\)—a -NZ$69.06B (+30.6%) negative additionality gap (consisting of -NZ$7.42B intra-lane and -NZ$61.64B cross-lane interference).

View B presents two complementary, mathematically exact decompositions of that gap:

1. Sequential Stack Cascade (View B, Left Waterfall): Stacks active lanes in canonical order \(k_1 < k_2 < \dots < k_m\): \[ V(S) = \underbrace{\sum_{r=1}^{m} \sum_{i \in S_{k_r}} V(\{i\})}_{\text{Siloed Sum}} + \underbrace{\sum_{r=1}^{m} \Big(V(S_{k_r}) - \sum_{i \in S_{k_r}} V(\{i\})\Big)}_{\text{Wedge 1: Intra-Lane}} + \sum_{r=2}^{m} \underbrace{\Big[V\!\Big(\bigcup_{j=1}^{r} S_{k_j}\Big) - V\!\Big(\bigcup_{j=1}^{r-1} S_{k_j}\Big) - V(S_{k_r})\Big]}_{\text{Sequential Cross-Lane Wedge } r}. \]
2. Order-Invariant \(2^m\) Shapley-Owen Attribution (View B, Right Bars): Averages each lane's marginal impact across all \(m!\) entry orders—equivalently dividing every Harsanyi synergy dividend \(m(T) = \sum_{R \subseteq T} (-1)^{|T|-|R|} V\!\big(\bigcup_{r \in R} S_r\big)\) equally among the \(|T|\) participating lanes: \[ \phi_r(V) = \sum_{Q \subseteq \mathcal{L}_{\text{act}} \setminus \{r\}} \frac{|Q|!\,(m - |Q| - 1)!}{m!} \Big[ V(Q \cup \{r\}) - V(Q) \Big] = V(S_r) + \sum_{\substack{T \subseteq \mathcal{L}_{\text{act}} \\ r \in T,\, |T| \ge 2}} \frac{m(T)}{|T|}, \qquad \sum_{r=1}^{m} \phi_r(V) = V(S). \]

Why the Two Decompositions Differ Numerically

  • Eliminating First-Mover Asymmetry: In the sequential waterfall (L1 → L2 → L3 → L4), Lane 1 enters onto an empty stage and receives NZ$0.00B of cross-lane wedge while Lane 2 absorbs 100% of their bilateral Rent × ECE collision (-NZ$21.21B). Shapley splits every bilateral interaction 50/50 and every 3-way interaction 1/3 each, revealing that Lane 1 (Children, Health & Education) actually carries the largest proportional cross-economy amplification (+51.0%, from -NZ$47.57B siloed to -NZ$71.81B in GE).
  • Splitting the Quadratic Sovereign Debt Cross-Term: Along Document 3's macroeconomic welfare surface, sovereign debt crowding-out above 42% of GDP and distortionary PAYE closure above 22% are strictly convex: \[ \Delta\text{CE}_{\text{Surf}}(D_t, \tau_{l,t}) = -0.045\,\max(0, D_t - 42) - 0.00032\,\max(0, D_t - 42)^2 - 0.920\,(\tau_{l,t} - 22) - 0.038\,\max(0, \tau_{l,t} - 22)^2. \] Because \((\Delta D_{\text{retrench}} + \Delta D_{\text{tax}})^2 = \Delta D_{\text{retrench}}^2 + \Delta D_{\text{tax}}^2 + 2\,\Delta D_{\text{retrench}}\Delta D_{\text{tax}}\), stacking C09 tax cuts last (Wedge 4 = -NZ$17.34B) bills 100% of that quadratic cross-term to Lane 4, whereas Shapley (-NZ$10.73B on Lane 4) attributes half of the cross-term back to Lanes 1–3 for eroding the fiscal base simultaneously.

5. Snowball Debt Dynamics & The 5 Public-Finance Accounting Lenses

Annual Crown operating balance deltas \(\Delta\text{OBEGAL}_t\) map onto Net Core Crown Debt (% of GDP) over \(t \in \{2023,\dots,2065\}\) via the lower-triangular \(r - g = 1.65\%\text{/yr}\) compounding operator \(\mathbf{C}\) atop the 200.0%-of-GDP 2065 LTFM baseline:

\[ D_t = D_t^{\text{LTFM}} + \sum_{s=2023}^{t} (1.0165)^{t - s} \left( -\frac{100 \cdot \Delta\text{OBEGAL}_s}{\text{GDP}_s} \right), \qquad \text{PV}_r = \int_{2024}^{2065} \beta_r(t)\,\Delta W_t\,dt. \]

Over the 42-year window (2024–2065 at 2.5% real), the Present Value of New Zealand's real GDP is NZ$15,869B (and PV household consumption is NZ$9,204B). Thus the -NZ$294.67B Full-16 general-equilibrium sinkhole represents 1.86% of 42-year PV GDP (or 3.20% of PV household consumption).
