## Part I: Targeted Executive Summaries

### 1. For Philanthropists: Catalytic Impact & The Intergenerational Multiplier
**Strategic Mandate:** Shift from funding late-stage symptom remediation to unlocking early-stage human potential where private markets and family balance sheets fail[cite: 2, 3].

* **The Core Problem (Early Stunting & The Metropolitan Squeeze):** Across New Zealand's four domestic geographic tiers—**Rural** districts (16.0% of households), **Small** provincial towns (24.0%), **Large** regional cities (25.9%), and **Auckland** (34.1%)—low-income families face an acute early-childhood investment trap. Because young parents cannot borrow against their children's future adult earnings and face severe housing cost pressures in **Large** cities and **Auckland**, cash-poor families are forced to cut private early-childhood enrichment to the bone[cite: 3, 7]. In our baseline model simulations, a family's economic position when parents are aged 30 to 35 locks in **58.8% (Rural) to 85.8% (Auckland)** of the variation in their children's adult human capital, and **70.7% (Rural) to 88.7% (Auckland)** of their children's future lifetime earnings[cite: 9].
* **The Catalytic Lever (Why the First 2000 Days Beat Late Remediation 3-to-1):** Childhood skill formation is a two-stage process where early foundational capabilities acquired before age 5 determine how much a child can absorb during primary and secondary school[cite: 1, 3, 6]. Expanding wraparound early childhood education and whanau support during the **First 2000 Days** (modeled on Barnardos' *Te Korowai Mokopuna*[cite: 3]) raises a child's readiness entering school, which in turn makes existing public schooling and parental effort dramatically more productive[cite: 1, 3, 6]. Our simulations show that front-loading investment in the First 2000 Days lifts long-run average lifetime earnings by **+10.6% in Rural districts**, **+20.0% in Small provincial towns**, **+11.2% in Large regional cities**, and **+3.9% in Auckland**—roughly **triple** the earnings payoff of spending the exact same public dollars during the school years (ages 6 to 17), which yields only +3.6%, +7.0%, +5.2%, and +1.3% respectively[cite: 3].
* **The Full Generational Payoff:** Pairing First-2000-Days wraparound support with a universal Youth Capital Grant at age 20 generates lifetime welfare gains equivalent to a permanent consumption increase of **+18.1% in Rural New Zealand**, **+18.6% in Small towns**, **+18.7% in Large cities**, and **+4.9% in Auckland**, while significantly narrowing metropolitan earnings inequality and lifting intergenerational mobility[cite: 3, 8, 9].

### 2. For Venture & Growth Capital Firms: Ending the Brain Drain & Reallocating Capital
**Strategic Mandate:** Unlock domestic productive capital trapped in overvalued urban land and stem the outward emigration of top-tier young talent to Australia and the OECD[cite: 3, 4].

* **The Core Problem (Capital Misallocation & The Stepping-Stone Brain Drain):** New Zealand's innovation ecosystem is held back by two connected structural distortions:
  1. **Passive Land Hoarding Over Productive Scale-Ups:** Overvalued residential land in **Auckland** (where 35.1% of New Zealand businesses operate) and **Large** regional metros (26.9% of businesses) absorbs domestic savings, while high-productivity, collateral-constrained firms are starved of expansion capital and penalized by high corporate income taxes[cite: 3].
  2. **The Two-Stage Talent Funnel to Australia:** Our spatial migration analysis across **Rural**, **Small**, **Large**, **Auckland**, and **Abroad** reveals a two-stage brain drain. Ambitious youth from **Rural** and **Small** provincial towns use **Large** university and service cities (Wellington, Christchurch, Hamilton, Tauranga, Dunedin) as a domestic stepping stone, while **Large** cities and **Auckland** suffer direct outward brain drain of top-tier young graduates who lack inherited family property deposits and face compressed after-housing incomes at home[cite: 3, 4].
* **Strategic Levers for Venture & Growth Leaders:**
  1. **Champion a Pro-Productivity Tax Shift:** Shifting the tax burden off corporate and capital income and onto a modest book-value wealth/land levy (1.5%) and estate tax (25%) on the **\$1.6 trillion intergenerational property transfer** creates a powerful "use-it-or-lose-it" incentive[cite: 3, 4]. Capital rotates out of passive property holding into high-return operating firms, lifting aggregate productivity and boosting domestic wages by **+3.2% in Rural districts to +5.5% in Auckland**[cite: 3].
  2. **Build in Secondary Urban Hubs (`Small` & `Large`):** As property overvaluation cools in Auckland (-7.5%) and Large regional metros (-5.0%) and young adults receive early-career capital seeding, **Small** provincial centers (+20.8% long-run earnings growth) and **Large** regional metros (+11.9% long-run earnings growth) experience the fastest surge in skilled workforce capacity[cite: 3].

### 3. For Policy-Makers: The "Income x Geography" Rule, Fertility Recovery, & Fiscal Solvency
**Strategic Mandate:** Replace one-size-fits-all national transfers with spatially tailored policy across New Zealand's four geographic tiers to reverse metropolitan fertility decline and secure the long-run fiscal solvency of New Zealand Superannuation[cite: 3].

* **Why a 4-Region Policy Design Matters:** Splitting secondary urban New Zealand into **Small** provincial towns (24.0% of households) and **Large** regional cities (25.9%) alongside **Rural** (16.0%) and **Auckland** (34.1%) exposes why uniform national welfare rules fall short[cite: 3]:
  * **Provincial Cost-of-Living vs. Metropolitan Housing Stress:** **Rural** and **Small** town households face elastic housing supply but heavy mandatory fuel and transport costs, requiring broad cost-of-living relief and universal early-childhood service delivery[cite: 3]. **Large** cities and **Auckland** face inelastic land supply, wide income gaps, and severe rental stress for young families, requiring targeted transfer progressivity and deposit relief[cite: 3].
  * **Reversing the Metropolitan Fertility Crunch:** High housing costs and the career penalty of childcare fall disproportionately on young urban mothers[cite: 3, 6]. In our baseline simulation, Auckland's Total Fertility Rate is crushed down to **1.42 children per woman**—and drops further to **1.18** when couples procrastinate under present-biased financial stress—while educated urban women forego or delay children at nearly triple the rate of low-income cohorts. Expanding wraparound First-2000-Days childcare and cooling urban housing costs lifts **Auckland's fertility rate from 1.42 back to 1.75** (and **Large** metro fertility from **3.57 to 3.68**), while cutting the socioeconomic fertility gap in Large cities by **28.6%**[cite: 3, 6].
  * **Securing NZ Superannuation Solvency:** Left unchecked, urban fertility delay pushes the aged dependency ratio (retirees aged 65+ per working-age adult) up to **14.8%**. Implementing the full reform package—combining First-2000-Days wraparound support, housing affordability reform, and family commitment support—reduces the long-run 65+ dependency ratio to **11.2%** (a **24.3% structural improvement**), stabilizing the required labor tax rate at **28.1%** while funding universal early-childhood investment[cite: 3].

### 4. For Economists: Advancing the Quantitative Macroeconomics Frontier
**Research Contribution:** A GPU-accelerated Mixed Finite/Infinite-Horizon 4-Region + Abroad Spatial Overlapping Generations (OLG) model in the **VFI Toolkit** that unifies four major macroeconomic frameworks without suffering from the curse of dimensionality[cite: 2, 3, 6, 7]:

1. **Mixed Finite/Infinite-Horizon Multi-Region Equilibrium:** Simultaneously solves 4 finite-horizon regional household sectors (ages 20 to 99 across Rural, Small, Large, and Auckland) and 4 infinite-horizon collateral-constrained entrepreneurial firm sectors on the GPU[cite: 3, 7].
2. **Two-Stage Childhood Skill Experience Asset + Adult Ben-Porath Training:** Embeds Cunha, Heckman, and Schennach's (2010) and Lee and Seshadri's (2019) multi-stage childhood skill formation alongside Fan, Seshadri, and Taber's (2022) adult on-the-job training using off-grid linear interpolation[cite: 1, 3, 6].
3. **Dimension-Free Housing Tenure & Unbalanced Growth Land Bubbles:** Combines Kiyotaki, Michaelides, and Nikolov's (2024) net-worth consolidation and analytical homeownership cutoffs with Hirano and Toda's (2025) unbalanced sectoral growth across all four regions with zero additional state variables[cite: 3, 7].
4. **Endogenous Domestic Stepping-Stone & International Brain-Drain Matrix:** Captures both domestic youth sorting across the four New Zealand regions and selective outward emigration to Australia/OECD, dynamically updating regional population and business shares over a 60-year transition horizon[cite: 3, 4].

### Lens 1: Early Childhood Skill Formation & Family Fertility Decisions
* **Why Early Investment Amplifies Schooling:** During the **First 2000 Days** (ages 0 to 5), a child's developing cognitive and non-cognitive capabilities are highly malleable, allowing high-quality wraparound early childhood education and whanau support to compensate for family material hardship[cite: 1, 3, 6]. By the time a child enters primary and secondary school (ages 6 to 17), learning becomes cumulative: children who arrive at age 5 with strong foundational self-regulation and language skills gain far more from every classroom hour[cite: 1, 3, 6]. Consequently, public funds invested before age 5 crowd *in* parental effort during the school years, whereas late remedial spending struggles to overcome early developmental gaps[cite: 1, 3, 6].
* **Housing Costs, Childcare Bargaining, & Fertility Procrastination:** When couples decide whether to have children, they weigh the lifelong joy of parenthood against immediate housing space costs and the career earnings penalty borne primarily by mothers[cite: 3, 6]. In **Auckland** and **Large** regional cities, steep rents and mortgage payments combine with childcare costs to cause young couples to delay starting families—a pattern worsened when immediate financial pressures induce procrastination[cite: 3, 6]. Universal wraparound early-childhood support directly reduces this maternal career penalty, allowing urban families to achieve their intended family size[cite: 3].

### Lens 2: The 4-Region Spatial Divide & The Brain-Drain Funnel
* **New Zealand's Four Distinct Economic Tiers:**
  * **Rural Districts (16.0% of Households / 15.0% of Businesses):** Characterized by responsive land supply and affordable housing, lower wage ceilings, and the country's highest mandatory household spending on fuel and transport[cite: 3].
  * **Small Provincial Towns (24.0% of Households / 23.0% of Businesses):** Moderate housing costs, compressed middle-income wage distributions, and elevated transport burdens[cite: 3].
  * **Large Regional Cities (25.9% of Households / 26.9% of Businesses — Wellington, Christchurch, Hamilton, Tauranga, Dunedin):** Constrained urban land, higher knowledge and professional wages, and lower transport shares, serving as New Zealand's primary domestic magnet for university students and young professionals from provincial areas[cite: 3].
  * **Auckland (34.1% of Households / 35.1% of Businesses):** New Zealand's primate metropolitan economy, featuring the highest business productivity ceiling and widest wage dispersion alongside severely constrained land supply and acute housing crowd-out for low-income renters[cite: 3, 7].
* **Career Skill Maintenance & The Emigration Margin:** Workers who enter the labor force at age 20 with strong foundational skills continuously upgrade their capabilities through on-the-job training, whereas workers who were stunted in childhood experience flat wages and early skill decay in their 50s[cite: 3, 6]. At the top of the skill distribution, ambitious 20-to-35-year-olds in **Large** cities and **Auckland** who do not inherit family property deposits compare their squeezed domestic living standards against higher wages and more affordable housing in Australia and the OECD, driving New Zealand's persistent outward brain drain[cite: 3, 4].

### Lens 3: The \$1.6 Trillion Property Transfer, Productive Firms, & Superannuation
* **Reallocating Capital from Passive Land to Productive Firms:** Over the next four decades, approximately **\$1.6 trillion in residential property wealth**—heavily concentrated in Auckland and Large regional cities—will pass from retiring cohorts to the next generation[cite: 3, 4, 7]. Under current tax settings, untaxed property gains and high corporate income taxes encourage capital to sit in passive residential land rather than financing innovative operating businesses[cite: 3, 7]. Shifting the tax mix toward a modest annual wealth/land levy and an estate tax on large inheritances releases capital to high-growth businesses, raises domestic wages, cools urban house-price-to-income ratios, and funds both First-2000-Days wraparound ECE and a universal Youth Capital Grant[cite: 3, 7].

## **Part IV: Methodological Defense & Mathematical Appendix**

This appendix provides the formal mathematical defense of the modeling choices, identification strategies, and computational reductions used to construct the New Zealand 4-Region Intergenerational Suite.

### **A.1 Defending the Mixed Finite/Infinite-Horizon PType Architecture**

**The Critique:** *Why not model entrepreneurs as finite-horizon life-cycle households, or model all households as infinitely lived dynastic agents?*  
**The Defense:**

1. In standard Aiyagari life-cycle models, households save in a single risk-free bond/capital pool rented to a static representative firm ($Y = Z K^\alpha L^{1-\alpha}$). In that setup, individual portfolio returns are identical ($r$), so a book-value wealth tax $\tau_a$ and a capital income tax $\tau_k$ are mathematically isomorphic ($\tau_a = r \tau_k$), completely missing the **Guvenen et al. (2023) capital misallocation channel**.  
2. Conversely, if heterogeneous entrepreneurial returns $R(z_f)$ are attached directly to finite-horizon households, retirees at ages 75–95 are forced to operate collateral-constrained firms, confounding life-cycle retirement decumulation (Fan, Seshadri, & Taber; De Nardi, 2004) with corporate equity dynamics.  
3. By partitioning the economy in the VFI Toolkit into Names_i = {'HH_Rural', 'HH_Small', 'HH_Large', 'HH_Auckland', 'Firm_Rural', 'Firm_Small', 'Firm_Large', 'Firm_Auckland'} with N_j.HH_k = 16 and N_j.Firm_k = Inf, finite-horizon households supply labor and bond/mutual-fund savings (a_h), while infinite-horizon firms face persistent productivity shocks $z_f \in \lbrace z_{\ell,k}, z_{h,k} \rbrace$, borrow up to collateral limit $k \le \lambda_{\mathrm{col}} a_f$, and solve:  

$$
V_{F,k}(a_f, z_f) = \max_{a^{\prime}_f \in \lbrack 0, R_k(z_f)a_f \rbrack} \left\lbrace \ln\left\lparen R_k(z_f) a_f - a^{\prime}_f \right\rparen + \beta_{\mathrm{firm}} \sum_{z^{\prime}_f} \Pi_{f,k}(z^{\prime}_f \mid z_f) V_{F,k}(a^{\prime}_f, z^{\prime}_f) \right\rbrace
$$

where:

$$
R_k(z_f) = (1 - \tau_a)^5 + (1 - \tau_k) \left\lparen r + \max \left\lbrace 0, \alpha_k \left\lparen \frac{1-\alpha_k}{w_k} \right\rparen^{\frac{1-\alpha_k}{\alpha_k}} z_f - (r + \delta_k) \right\rbrace \lambda_{\mathrm{col}} \right\rparen
$$

Because VFI Toolkit loops over Names_i sequentially on the GPU, this mixed-horizon synthesis adds **zero state variables** to the household grid while preserving the exact analytical properties of Guvenen et al.

### **A.2 Defending the Nonparametric Skill Anchoring & Luxury In-Kind Specification**

**The Critique:** *Early childhood test scores have arbitrary scales and are ridden with measurement error; furthermore, standard benefit-incidence studies value public ECE at \$1 per \$1 of spending, which overstates welfare gains for cash-poor families.*  
**The Defense:**

1. **Adult Outcome Anchoring & Measurement Error Correction:** Following Cunha, Heckman, and Schennach (2010), latent childhood skills $\theta_{k,t}$ are anchored directly in cardinal adult earnings/schooling outcomes $g_{k,j}(\theta_{k,t})$ ($h \in H$ at age 20). As Cunha et al. demonstrate in Tables I–III, ignoring measurement error in early skill proxies severely attenuates Stage 1 investment loadings and overstates late-stage substitutability; our calibration directly adopts their measurement-error-corrected factor loadings ($\gamma_{1,C,1} = 0.487, \gamma_{2,C,1} = 0.902$).

**Welfare-Based In-Kind Imputation ($\omega_i$):** Following Ferriere et al. (2025), standard monetary imputation values public in-kind spending $G_{\mathrm{ECE}}$ at full cost across all deciles, overstating redistributive compression by up to 50% because constrained families at the corner solution $i_c = 0$ have a high marginal utility of cash $\partial u_i / \partial y_i$. In Calibrate_NZ_Moments.m, we discipline the Stone-Geary luxury parameter $\bar{g}_{\mathrm{ECE}}$ against the empirical share $S = \Pr(i_c = 0) \approx 0.25$ of fertile households making zero private top-ups, and apply Ferriere et's exact welfare-based imputation weight:

$$
\omega_i = \frac{\partial u_i / \partial G_{\mathrm{ECE}}}{\partial u_i / \partial y_i} = \min \left\lbrace \frac{y_i / \bar{y}}{F^{-1}(S)}, 1 \right\rbrace
$$

2. Even under this strict discounting of direct utility ($\omega_i < 1$ in $Q_1, Q_2$), public $G_{\mathrm{ECE}}$ generates massive positive welfare gains (CE % > 0) via the concave societal externality $X_t$ and the intergenerational human capital transition matrix $Q_h$.

### **A.3 Defending the Spatial & Housing Dimensionality Reductions**

**The Critique:** *Modeling 4 regions (Rural, Small, Large, Auckland), liquid bonds $b$, physical housing stock $H$, and binary tenure choice (Rent vs. Own) would normally multiply the endogenous state space by $4 \times N_H \times 2$, causing GPU memory overflow.*  
**The Defense:**  
We achieve exact spatial and housing representation with **zero extra endogenous grid states** via three structural reductions:

1. **Horizontal Spatial Partitioning (Names_i):** Mapping $k \in \lbrace \mathrm{Rural}, \mathrm{Small}, \mathrm{Large}, \mathrm{Auckland} \rbrace$ to VFI Toolkit Permanent Types (Names_i) means each region is solved sequentially on the GPU with its own local wage $w_k$, house price $Q_k$, income dispersion $\sigma_z(k)$, and mandatory fuel/transport subsistence $\bar{e}_k$.  
2. **Net-Worth Consolidation ($a = Q_k h_{\mathrm{house}} - b_{\mathrm{debt}}$):** Following Kiyotaki, Michaelides, and Nikolov (2024), when young homeowners borrow up to the collateral limit, financial debt $b$ and physical housing $h_{\mathrm{house}}$ consolidate into a single state variable: net worth $a \ge 0$. Intra-period expenditure $e_{\mathrm{tot}}$ splits analytically between non-durable consumption (share $\phi_{\mathrm{nd}}$) and housing space (share $1 - \phi_{\mathrm{nd}}$).

**Closed-Form Tenure Cutoff ($\xi_k$) & Unbalanced Growth Bubble ($G_k$):** Given regional trend growth $G_k$ (Hirano & Toda, 2025), local rental yields:
 
$$
rr_k = \left\lparen 1 + \mu_{\mathrm{maint}} - \frac{G_k}{(1+r)^{1/5}} \right\rparen Q_k
$$

and homeowner user costs:

$$
rh_k = \left\lparen 1 + \mu_{\mathrm{maint}} - \upsilon_h - \frac{G_k}{(1+r)^{1/5}} \right\rparen Q_k
$$

pin down the exact fraction of young households choosing leveraged ownership via the uniform preference draw $\chi \sim U \lbrack 0, \bar{\chi} \rbrack$:

$$
\xi_k = \min \left\lbrace 1, \frac{1}{\bar{\chi}} \left\lparen \frac{rr_k}{rh_k} \right\rparen \right\rbrace
$$

3. Regional housing markets clear in General Equilibrium against elastic supply $HS(k) = H_k (Q_k)^{\delta_{H,k}}$, where $\delta_{H,\mathrm{Auckland}} = 0.10 < \delta_{H,\mathrm{Large}} = 0.18 < \delta_{H,\mathrm{Small}} = 0.28 < \delta_{H,\mathrm{Rural}} = 0.35$.

### **A.4 Defending Warm-Glow + Transition Matrix vs. Explicit Dynastic VFI**

**The Critique:** *Why use a warm-glow bequest function $u_{\mathrm{beq}}(a^{\prime})$ and log child-skill payoff $\omega_{ic} \ln(h_{\mathrm{child}})$ inside the household Bellman equation rather than solving an outer Barro–Becker Dynastic VFI loop over $V_{\mathrm{child}}$?*  
**The Defense:**

1. **Distributional Equivalence:** As De Nardi (2004) establishes, embedding a child's full multi-dimensional state vector inside the parent's Bellman equation expands the state space and introduces strategic parent–child gaming, whereas a two-parameter luxury warm-glow function $(b_1, b_2)$ accurately reproduces the upper tail of the estate distribution and old-age saving profiles.  
2. **Capturing the 75% Intergenerational Multiplier:** In Daruich (2018, Section 6), three-fourths of the long-run welfare gain from early-childhood policy operates through the shift in the **stationary distribution of parent states $\mu(a, h, z)$** across generations, rather than changes in the curvature of the continuation value function. Because Build_IntergenTransitionMatrix.m maps the exact optimal child investments $i_c^{\ast}(a_p, h_p, z_p, k)$ and net-of-tax estates $(1 - \tau_b) a_j^{\prime \ast}(k)$ into the next generation's initial distribution jequaloneDist ($\mu_{1,k}(a_1, h_1, z_1)$), our architecture captures 100% of this macroeconomic dynastic feedback loop.  
3. **Optional 2-Parameter Dynastic Slope Toggle:** Because a newborn's expected value function $E_{a_1, z_1} \lbrack V_k(a_1, h_1, z_1, 1) \rbrack$ in a CRRA economy is smooth and concave in initial human capital $h_1$, our log-skill payoff $\omega_{ic} \ln(h_{\mathrm{child}})$ is a tight functional proxy for $E \lbrack V_1 \rbrack$. When evaluating reforms that drastically alter future adult tax progressivity (where farsighted parents might adjust $i_c$ in anticipation of their child's altered after-tax return to skill), one can toggle on a 4-iteration outer loop fitting:

$$
E_{a_1, z_1} \lbrack V_k(a_1, h_1, z_1, 1) \rbrack \approx \hat{\alpha}_{0,k} + \hat{\alpha}_{1,k} \frac{h_1^{1-\eta_{\mathrm{dyn}}}}{1-\eta_{\mathrm{dyn}}}
$$

(following Lee & Seshadri's $V_4^n \rightarrow V_4^{n+1}$ contraction) with zero change to grid dimensions.
