## **Part I: Targeted Executive Summaries**

### **1. For Philanthropists: Catalytic Impact & The Intergenerational Multiplier**

**Strategic Mandate:** Shift from funding late-stage symptom remediation to unlocking early-stage human potential where private markets and family balance sheets fail.

* **The Core Problem (Early Stunting & The Metropolitan Squeeze):** In New Zealand, roughly **14.3% to 22%** of each birth cohort grows up in severe material hardship or developmental stunting. The landmark Dunedin Multidisciplinary Health and Development Study demonstrates that 22% of a cohort—identifiable as early as age 3—accounts for **57% to 81%** of adult hospital nights, welfare benefits, and criminal convictions. Because young parents cannot borrow against their children's future adult earnings and face severe housing stress in metropolitan centers like **Auckland** (34.1% of the population) and **Large** regional cities (25.9%), low-income families hit an economic wall where private early-childhood enrichment collapses to zero.  
* **Why Cash Transfers Alone Fall Short:** Economic research by Ferriere et al. (2025) proves that early childhood education, preventative health, and mental health support operate as "luxury goods" at the household budget level. When families are crushed by metropolitan rent or provincial transport costs, unrestricted cash is absorbed by immediate survival needs rather than developmental inputs. Direct, in-kind wraparound provision—modeled on Barnardos' *Te Korowai Mokopuna* First 2000 Days framework—bypasses this household budget constraint, lifting children off the developmental floor and generating a massive societal payoff.
* **The Catalytic Lever (Two-Stage Dynamic Complementarity):** Building on Nobel Laureate James Heckman's skill formation framework (Cunha, Heckman, & Schennach, 2010), our model proves that capabilities acquired in the **First 2000 Days** (ages 0 to 5) directly raise the productivity of all subsequent primary and secondary schooling (ages 6 to 17). Scaling wraparound early childhood provision crowds *in* private parental effort during the school years, capturing a **NZD 112 to NZD 162 billion** present-value societal payoff—roughly triple the return of late school-age remediation.
* **The Risk of Mid-Childhood Fade-Out:** Unlocking human potential in the First 2,000 Days is necessary, but it is not self-insuring against adolescent family trauma[cite: 6]. Longitudinal evidence from New Zealand's *Growing Up in New Zealand* ("Now We Are 12") cohort shows that 22% of children experience material hardship in their first twelve years, and more than half of those affected encounter acute hardship at age 12—often triggered by parental job loss, income shocks, or metropolitan housing stress in Auckland and Large regional cities[cite: 6]. Because early childhood skills and school-age investments are strong complements, an unbuffered family unemployment crisis at ages 12 to 14 erodes early developmental gains and pushes vulnerable youth back toward the stunted trajectory and its four costly exits (entry into state care, avoidable hospitalisations, lower lifetime income, and benefit or justice system contact)[cite: 6].
* **The Two-Way Montessori Mentoring Dividend:** While pure academic remediation becomes costly and less effective in early adolescence, socio-emotional and executive-function skills remain highly malleable between ages 12 and 14[cite: 6, 17]. Drawing on Montessori's multi-age educational architecture (*Erdkinder*) and recent randomized mentoring trials, scaling structured cross-age peer mentoring—where 12-to-14-year-olds are trained and trusted to mentor younger children—unlocks an extraordinary two-way generational dividend:
  1. **Downward Scaffolding for Younger Children (Ages 0–8):** Near-peer modeling and tutoring amplify the effectiveness of public early childhood and primary wraparound support, accelerating foundational language, executive function, and school readiness.
  2. **Upward "Gain Banking" for Adolescent Mentors (Ages 12–14):** Conferring status, autonomy, and prosocial purpose on early adolescents acts as a psychological and executive-function buffer against household unemployment stress. By serving as mentors, adolescents consolidate ("bank") their First-2,000-Days gains, preventing downward mobility and lifting Auckland's thriving youth cohort share from 40.5% to 55.3% while cutting the stunted trajectory from 17.9% to below 1%[cite: 6].
* **The Three-Fourths Generational Multiplier:** Quantitative modeling (Daruich, 2018) confirms that **55% to 75% of the long-run welfare gain** from early childhood intervention comes not just from making a child more productive in their own lifetime, but from the *intergenerational multiplier*: a thriving child matures into a higher-skilled, higher-earning parent who naturally invests more time and resources into the *next* generation.

### **2. For Venture & Growth Capital Firms: Ending the Brain Drain & Reallocating Capital**

**Strategic Mandate:** Unlock domestic productive capital trapped in overvalued urban land and stem the outward emigration of top-tier talent to Australia and the OECD.

* **The Core Problem (Capital Misallocation & The Stepping-Stone Brain Drain):** Venture and growth firms thrive on two inputs: **high-talent innovators** and **patient, productive capital**. New Zealand's status quo starves both. Over the next 40 years, an estimated **NZD 1.6 trillion** in wealth will transfer across generations, overwhelmingly locked in overvalued residential land. Passive wealth holders park capital in property and low-yield collateral, starving high-growth, collateral-constrained businesses of expansion capital.
* **Plugging the Adolescent Leak in Technical & Executive Talent:** High-growth enterprises in Auckland (35.1% of New Zealand businesses) and Large regional hubs (26.9% of businesses) depend on a deep domestic pipeline of cognitively agile, emotionally resilient young adults[cite: 6]. When family employment shocks and urban housing costs destabilize youth at ages 12 to 14, New Zealand loses prospective founders, engineers, and skilled tradespeople a decade before they ever reach the labor market[cite: 6, 8].
* **The "Protégé Effect" as Early Leadership Formation:** Cross-age Montessori peer mentoring at ages 12 to 14 is the earliest, lowest-cost incubator of non-cognitive leadership capital—agency, communication, self-regulation, and collaborative problem-solving[cite: 17]. Combined with the tax mix shift away from productive enterprise toward passive land holdings (cooling metropolitan housing overvaluation by 5.0% to 7.5% and lifting regional wages by 3.2% to 5.5%), banking adolescent skills expands the pool of thriving 20-to-35-year-olds retained in New Zealand and cuts high-skill brain drain to Australia and the OECD nearly in half[cite: 6].
* **The Two-Stage Talent Funnel to Australia:** New Zealand suffers acute outward migration among educated 20-to-35-year-olds. Mapping migration across **Rural** districts (16.0% of households), **Small** provincial towns (24.0%), **Large** regional metros (25.9%), **Auckland** (34.1%), and **Abroad** reveals a two-stage funnel: high-potential youth from **Rural** and **Small** provincial towns use **Large** university and service metros (Wellington, Christchurch, Hamilton, Tauranga, Dunedin) as a stepping stone, while **Large** cities and **Auckland** (where 35.1% of NZ businesses reside) suffer the sharpest direct brain drain of top-tier talent to Australia and the OECD. Ambitious young adults who do not inherit family property deposits leave because overvalued Auckland and Large-city housing and capital-starved local firms depress their domestic real return.  
* **Integration into Venture Strategic Plans:**  
  1. **Champion a "Use-It-or-Lose-It" Tax Shift:** Standard corporate profit taxes punish successful innovators: the more productive the venture, the higher the tax bite. Following Guvenen et al. (2023), shifting the tax mix off active corporate income and onto a modest book-value wealth/land levy (1.5%) and estate tax (25%) on the **NZD 1.6T intergenerational property transfer** creates a powerful **use-it-or-lose-it effect**. Passive property hoarders face a higher holding cost, rotating capital toward high-performing entrepreneurs, raising aggregate productivity, and lifting regional wages by **+3.2% to +5.5%**.  
  2. **Back Early-Adulthood Seed Endowments:** Recycling estate tax revenues into a universal **Youth Capital Grant** at age 20 provides first-generation founders and young professionals with the seed equity needed to start businesses or secure housing without relying on inherited family property.
  3. **Leverage Secondary Urban Hubs:** Exploit the improved housing affordability and rising skill retention in **Large** regional metros and **Small** provincial centers as metropolitan land overvaluation cools.

### **3. For Policy-Makers: The "Income $\times$ Geography" Rule & Fiscal Solvency**

**Strategic Mandate:** Replace one-size-fits-all national transfers with spatially tailored policy across New Zealand's four geographic tiers to secure the long-run fiscal solvency of New Zealand Superannuation and restore social cohesion.

* **The Demographic Vise & The Replacement Migration Churn Trap:** As the ratio of working-age citizens per retiree (65+) falls from ~3.9 toward 2.8 by 2051 and 2.0 by 2078, universal New Zealand Superannuation places immense pressure on the Crown budget. Relying on high replacement immigration (+25,000 net per year) to plug the hole left by emigrating native youth maintains headcount on paper but creates a costly macroeconomic leak. The Crown pays the full upfront cost of early childhood, primary, and tertiary education for native children (ages 0–19), exports their peak tax-paying years abroad, and absorbs high population turnover that erodes local **Social Cohesion** and inflicts relational distress on aging parents whose children leave for opportunity.
* **Why a 4-Region Policy Design Matters:** Splitting secondary urban New Zealand into **Small** provincial towns (24.0%) and **Large** regional metros (25.9%) alongside **Rural** (16.0%) and **Auckland** (34.1%) exposes why uniform national welfare rules fail:  
  1. **Rural & Small Provincial Towns:** Exhibit compressed middle-income distributions, responsive housing supply, and heavy mandatory fuel and transport burdens (where rural households spend 2.7 to 2.8 times more of their budget on fuel than metropolitan residents). They require broad transport/cost-of-living relief and universal in-kind early-childhood service delivery.  
  2. **Large Regional Cities & Auckland:** Exhibit wide income gaps (deep material hardship alongside high-earning knowledge workers), constrained land supply, and severe rental crowd-out. Following Labrousse and Perdereau (2025), increasing transfer progressivity in **Large** cities and **Auckland** while funding a universal **Youth Capital Grant** yields the largest median welfare jump and cuts the national share of households experiencing welfare losses by 10 percentage points.
* **Moving from Single-Gate to Dual-Gate Prevention:** The 5-stage Child Fates Sankey analysis demonstrates why social investment must pair **Gate 1 (First 2,000 Days wraparound)** with **Gate 2 (Age 12–14 Adolescent Buffering & Mentoring)**[cite: 6]. In the status quo baseline, an observable parental unemployment shock during early adolescence triggers family distress that diverts children out of the thriving and core skill tiers into downstream fiscal liabilities[cite: 6].
* **High-Leverage Fiscal Protection:** Integrating Montessori-style cross-age peer mentoring into intermediate and junior secondary schools (Years 8–10) alongside Geography-by-Income family transfers insulates 12-to-14-year-olds when a parent loses work[cite: 6, 8]. Because the same program simultaneously boosts early learning in primary classrooms and prevents adolescent disengagement, it delivers a double fiscal dividend—shrinking annual government outlays on state care, acute hospital admissions, and youth justice while expanding the long-run tax base that sustains universal New Zealand Superannuation (reducing the 65+ dependency ratio from 12.95% to 11.19%)[cite: 6].

**Research Contribution:** A GPU-accelerated Mixed Finite/Infinite-Horizon 4-Region + Abroad Spatial Overlapping Generations (OLG) framework that bridges four foundational macroeconomic literatures without succumbing to the curse of dimensionality:

1. **Mixed Household & Firm Equilibrium:** Simultaneously solves life-cycle households (ages 20 to 99 across Rural, Small, Large, and Auckland) alongside infinite-horizon, collateral-constrained entrepreneurial firms (Guvenen et al., 2023) to capture both intergenerational human capital dynamics and corporate capital reallocation.  
2. **Multi-Stage Skill Formation & Career Maintenance:** Unifies Cunha, Heckman, and Schennach's (2010) and Lee and Seshadri's (2019) two-stage childhood skill technology with adult on-the-job training (Fan, Seshadri, & Taber, 2022) and Ferriere et al.'s (2025) non-homothetic in-kind public provision theory.  
3. **Advancing the Skill-Formation Frontier:** Standard overlapping-generations (OLG) models of childhood skill formation (Cunha, Heckman, & Schennach, 2010; Daruich, 2018; Lee & Seshadri, 2019) model vertical transmission from parent to child in isolation, omitting horizontal cross-age spillovers across childhood cohorts[cite: 6, 7, 17]. We extend the two-stage Constant Elasticity of Substitution (CES) skill technology by introducing an equilibrium cross-cohort mentoring operator linking Stage 2 adolescents (ages 12–14) and Stage 1 young children (ages 0–5)[cite: 6, 17].
4. **Spatial Housing Bubbles & Tenure Cleavages:** Combines Labrousse and Perdereau's (2025) spatial inequality framework, Hirano and Toda's (2025) unbalanced sectoral growth land-bubble theorem, and Kiyotaki, Michaelides, and Nikolov's (2024) renter-versus-owner tenure cleavage across four domestic regions plus an endogenous emigration margin to Australia/OECD.  
5. **Dimension-Free General Equilibrium Implementation:** By modeling the downward mentoring multiplier on effective early public investment and the upward non-cognitive buffer against parental unemployment shocks as intra-generational equilibrium aggregates inside the intergenerational transition operator, we capture bidirectional peer spillovers (Lillard & Else-Quest, 2006; Agostinelli, Saharkhiz, & Wiswall, 2024; Resnjanskij et al., 2024) with **zero additional endogenous grid dimensions**[cite: 6, 7].
6. **Full Intergenerational Feedback:** Captures 100% of the dynastic human capital, property estate, and spatial migration multipliers across a 60-year transition horizon.

### **Lens 1: Early Childhood Skill Formation (First 2000 Days vs. Schooling) & Family Formation**

* **Why Early Investment Multiplies Schooling:** Human capabilities are built in sequential stages. During Stage 1 (the **First 2000 Days**, ages 0 to 5), cognitive and social-emotional plasticity is high, meaning public wraparound support can effectively offset family hardship. During Stage 2 (**Schooling**, ages 6 to 17), learning is strongly complementary: children who enter school at age 5 with solid foundational skills absorb classroom instruction rapidly, whereas children who arrive stunted fall progressively further behind. Consequently, front-loading public investment in the First 2000 Days crowds *in* parental effort during the school years, whereas late remedial spending struggles to close early gaps.  
* **Housing Stress & Fertility Delay:** Young couples balance the desire to have children against housing costs and the career earnings penalty of childcare. When metropolitan rents and mortgage burdens surge in **Large** cities and **Auckland**—and when financial stress induces procrastination—young adults delay or undershoot their intended family size, accelerating demographic aging.

### **Lens 2: The 4-Region Spatial Divide & The Brain-Drain Funnel**

* **The Spatial Gradient ($\mathrm{Rural} \rightarrow \mathrm{Small} \rightarrow \mathrm{Large} \rightarrow \mathrm{Auckland}$):**  
  * **Rural Districts (16.0% of Households / 15.0% of Businesses):** Responsive land supply keeps housing affordable ($1.05\times$ baseline), but wage ceilings are lower ($0.85\times$) and mandatory household spending on fuel and transport is the highest in the nation ($4.8\%$ of subsistence budget).  
  * **Small Provincial Towns (24.0% of Households / 23.0% of Businesses):** Moderate housing costs ($1.40\times$), compressed middle-income wages ($0.93\times$), and elevated transport burdens ($3.6\%$).  
  * **Large Regional Metros (25.9% of Households / 26.9% of Businesses — Wellington, Christchurch, Hamilton, Tauranga, Dunedin):** Constrained urban land ($2.05\times$ housing cost), higher professional and knowledge-sector wages ($1.05\times$), and lower transport burdens ($2.2\%$), serving as the primary domestic stepping stone for provincial youth.  
  * **Auckland Primate Agglomeration (34.1% of Households / 35.1% of Businesses — "Paris" Analog):** Severely constrained land supply and rapid knowledge-sector growth generate structural land overvaluation ($3.10\times$ housing cost), the widest income inequality, and acute rental crowd-out for low-income families.  
* **Career Skill Decay & Positive-Selection Emigration:** Workers who enter adulthood stunted experience flat wage profiles and early skill decay in their 50s, pushing them toward early labor force exit. Meanwhile, high-skilled 20-to-35-year-olds without inherited property deposits compare their housing-squeezed domestic living standards against higher real returns in Australia and the OECD, driving outward emigration at peak tax-paying age.

### **Lens 3: Pensions, The NZD 1.6T Property Transfer, & Capital Allocation**

* **Unlocking the Auckland & Metropolitan Tax Base:** Because Auckland house prices are nearly triple rural levels and 50% above Large regional metros, accurately accounting for Auckland's 34.1% population share (rising toward ~36.5% by Year 30) expands the national property estate and wealth tax base by **8% to 10%**. Taxing passive land hoarding and large estates funds both universal First-2000-Days wraparound ECE and a universal Youth Capital Grant without raising income tax rates on working families.
* **The Capital Reallocation Engine:** As Piketty and Saez (2013) observe, entrepreneurial talent is far more evenly distributed across a birth cohort than inherited wealth. Shifting the tax burden off active business profits and onto dormant property wealth prunes capital from low-yield land hoarding and reallocates it toward high-productivity operating businesses, expanding the national capital stock and lifting real wages across all four regions.

**Three Headline Takeaways from the Opportunity Index:**

1. **The Status Quo "Stay-and-Slide" Trap:** Under current policy settings, **50.1% to 66.8%** of New Zealand's pre-employment youth cohort **remain in New Zealand and fail to match their parents' age-30 living standard** (driven by metropolitan rent-to-income inflation and early childhood skill gaps). Only **26.2%** of Auckland youth and **26.5%** of Large regional city youth both stay in New Zealand and match or exceed their parents by age 30.
2. **In Large Regional Cities, Nearly Half of All Upward Mobility Requires Emigrating:** In **Large** university and service cities (Wellington, Christchurch, Hamilton, Tauranga, Dunedin), **19.5%** of the entire youth cohort leaves for Australia/OECD and achieves upward mobility abroad, compared to **26.5%** who achieve it domestically—meaning **42.4% of all young adults from Large regional cities who beat their parents' living standard have to leave New Zealand to do so**.
3. **The Reform Flips Domestic Opportunity Above 58%–68%+:** By combining First-2000-Days wraparound ECE, the Youth Capital Grant, and the property-to-productive-enterprise tax shift, the share of young Kiwis who **stay in New Zealand and equal or exceed their parents by age 30** more than doubles in metropolitan New Zealand—jumping from **26.5% to 58.6%** in Large regional cities and from **26.2% to 68.6%** in Auckland, while cutting the "stay-and-do-worse" cohort in Auckland by more than half (from **66.8% down to 28.6%**).
