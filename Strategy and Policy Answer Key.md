## **Part I: Targeted Executive Summaries**

### **1. For Philanthropists: Catalytic Impact & The Intergenerational Multiplier**

**Strategic Mandate:** Shift from funding late-stage symptom remediation to unlocking early-stage human potential where private markets fail[cite: 1].

* **The Core Problem:** In New Zealand, roughly **14.3% to 22%** of each birth cohort grows up in severe material hardship or developmental stunting[cite: 1]. The Dunedin Multidisciplinary Health and Development Study demonstrates that 22% of a cohort—identifiable as early as age 3—accounts for **57% to 81%** of adult hospital nights, welfare benefits, and criminal convictions[cite: 1]. Because young parents cannot borrow against their children's future adult earnings (an intergenerational borrowing constraint) and face severe housing stress in metropolitan centers like **Auckland** (34.1% of the population) and **Large** regional cities (25.9%), low-income families hit a mathematical "corner solution" ($i_c = 0$) where private early-childhood investment collapses[cite: 1].  
* **The Catalytic Lever (Two-Stage Dynamic Complementarity):** Incorporating Cunha, Heckman, and Schennach's (2010) two-stage CES skill technology ($\theta_c$ experience asset) proves that early investment in the First 2000 Days ($j = 3$, ages 0–5, where elasticity of substitution is high, $\varphi_1 = 0.35$) raises the marginal productivity of later schooling ($j = 4$, ages 6–17, where dynamic complementarity is strong, $\varphi_2 = -0.95$)[cite: 1]. Scaling wraparound early childhood provision ($G_{\mathrm{ECE}}$, modeled on Barnardos' *Te Korowai Mokopuna*) crowds *in* private parental effort during school years and captures a **\$112–\$162 billion** present-value societal payoff[cite: 1].
* **Quantitative Proof:** See [`Table4_4Region_LeeSeshadri_IGE.csv`](Output_NZ_4Region_Intergen/Table4_4Region_LeeSeshadri_IGE.csv), [`Table5_4Region_LeeSeshadri_Output_Welfare.csv`](Output_NZ_4Region_Intergen/Table5_4Region_LeeSeshadri_Output_Welfare.csv), and **Figure 1** below.

### **2. For Venture & Growth Capital Firms: Ending the Brain Drain & Reallocating Capital**

**Strategic Mandate:** Unlock domestic productive capital trapped in overvalued urban land and stem the positive-selection emigration of top-tier talent[cite: 1].

* **The Core Problem (The Stepping-Stone Brain Drain):** New Zealand suffers acute outward migration among educated 20-to-35-year-olds[cite: 1]. Our $4 \times 5$ spatial discrete-choice model (**Rural**, **Small**, **Large**, **Auckland** $\rightarrow$ **Abroad**) reveals a two-stage funnel: high-potential youth from **Rural** districts (16.0% pop) and **Small** provincial towns (24.0% pop) use **Large** university/service metros (Wellington, Christchurch, Hamilton, Tauranga, Dunedin) as a stepping stone, while **Large** cities and **Auckland** (where 35.1% of NZ businesses reside) suffer the sharpest direct brain drain of top human-capital tiers ($h_5 \mathrm{--} h_7$) to Australia/OECD[cite: 1]. Young adults who do not inherit family property deposits leave because overvalued Auckland and Large-city housing ($Q_{\mathrm{Auckland}} = 3.10, Q_{\mathrm{Large}} = 2.05$) and capital-starved local firms depress their domestic real return[cite: 1].

  2. **Leverage Secondary Urban Hubs:** Exploit the improved housing affordability and rising skill retention in **Large** regional metros and **Small** provincial centers as land overvaluation cools[cite: 1].

### **3. For Policy-Makers: The "Income $\times$ Geography" Rule & Fiscal Solvency**

**Strategic Mandate:** Replace uniform national transfers with spatially differentiated policy across New Zealand's four distinct geographic tiers to secure the long-run fiscal solvency of NZ Superannuation[cite: 1].

* **Why a 4-Region Taxonomy Matters:** Splitting "Urban" into **Small** provincial towns (24.0%) and **Large** regional metros (25.9%) alongside **Rural** (16.0%) and **Auckland** (34.1%) exposes why uniform welfare rules fail[cite: 1]:  
  1. **Rural & Small Provincial Towns:** Exhibit compressed income distributions ($Q_2 \mathrm{--} Q_4$), elastic housing supply ($\delta_H \in \lbrack 0.28, 0.35 \rbrack$), and high mandatory fuel/transport subsistence burdens ($\bar{e}_{\mathrm{Rural}} = 0.048, \bar{e}_{\mathrm{Small}} = 0.036$)[cite: 1]. They require broad transport/cost-of-living relief and universal in-kind ECE delivery ($G_{\mathrm{ECE}}$), with lower transfer progressivity[cite: 1].  
  2. **Large Regional Cities & Auckland:** Exhibit bifurcated income distributions ($Q_1$ hardship alongside $Q_5$ knowledge workers), inelastic land supply ($\delta_H \in \lbrack 0.10, 0.18 \rbrack$), and severe rental/deposit crowd-out[cite: 1]. Following Labrousse and Perdereau (2025), increasing transfer progressivity in **Large** cities and **Auckland** while funding a universal **Youth Capital Grant ($E$)** yields the largest median welfare jump (mirroring the +24.1% Large-city gain in Labrousse & Perdereau) and cuts the national welfare-loss share by 10 percentage points[cite: 1].

### **Lens 1: Early Childhood Skill Formation (First 2000 Days vs. Schooling) & Fertility**

Early childhood is the foundational engine of long-run macroeconomic productivity[cite: 1]. Drawing on Cunha, Heckman, and Schennach (2010), Daruich (2018), and Lee and Seshadri (2019), human capital is produced through a multi-stage nonlinear technology combining cognitive ($\theta_C$), noncognitive/mental health ($\theta_N, hm_t$), and physical health ($hp_t$) inputs[cite: 1]:

$$\theta_{k,t+1} = \left\lbrack \gamma_{s,k,1} \theta_{C,t}^{\varphi_{s,k}} + \gamma_{s,k,2} \theta_{N,t}^{\varphi_{s,k}} + \gamma_{s,k,3} I_{k,t}^{\varphi_{s,k}} + \gamma_{s,k,4} \theta_P^{\varphi_{s,k}} \right\rbrack^{\frac{1}{\varphi_{s,k}}} e^{\eta_{k,t}}$$

* **Two-Stage CES Experience Asset ($\theta_c$):** Children progress through Stage 1 (First 2000 Days, parental age $j = 3$, $\varphi_1 = 0.35$) and Stage 2 (Schooling, parental age $j = 4$, $\varphi_2 = -0.95$)[cite: 1]. Public front-loading in Stage 1 ($P_0$) raises $\theta_2$ entering school, which crowds *in* private parental investment during Stage 2, whereas late school-age subsidies ($P_1 / P_2$) fail to remediate early stunting[cite: 1].  
* **Non-Homothetic Luxury Goods & Pro-Equality Externalities:** Ferriere et al. (2025) show that education and preventative health have a Stone-Geary luxury threshold $\bar{g}_{\mathrm{ECE}} > 0$[cite: 1]:

$$i_c = \max \left\lbrace \frac{\omega}{\phi} \frac{1 - \tau}{1 - s} z_i - G_{\mathrm{ECE}} - \bar{g}_{\mathrm{ECE}}, 0 \right\rbrace$$

Because $\bar{g}_{\mathrm{ECE}} > 0$, low-income and housing-stressed parents below productivity cutoff $\bar{z}$ hit the corner solution $i_c = 0$[cite: 1]. Only direct in-kind wraparound provision $G_{\mathrm{ECE}}$ lifts disadvantaged children off the floor and maximizes the concave societal externality $X_t = \frac{\chi}{\alpha} \ln \left( \int \left( i_c + G_{\mathrm{ECE}} + \bar{g}_{\mathrm{ECE}} \right)^\alpha d\mu \right)$, eliminating the downstream fiscal drag of crime, hospitalization, and benefit dependency[cite: 1].
* **Endogenous Fertility & Hyperbolic Bargaining:** When metropolitan housing costs ($Q_{\mathrm{Large}}, Q_{\mathrm{Auckland}}$) surge or present-biased preferences ($\beta < 1$) bind, young adults delay or undershoot intended fertility, accelerating demographic aging (tracked in [`Table7_4Region_Endogenous_TFR_DiffFertility.csv`](Output_NZ_4Region_Intergen/Table7_4Region_Endogenous_TFR_DiffFertility.csv))[cite: 1].

### **Lens 2: The 4-Region Spatial Divide & The $4 \times 5$ Brain-Drain Funnel**

* **The Spatial Gradient ($\mathrm{Rural} \rightarrow \mathrm{Small} \rightarrow \mathrm{Large} \rightarrow \mathrm{Auckland}$):**  
  * **Rural (16.0% Pop / 15.0% Firms):** Elastic land ($\delta_H = 0.35, Q = 1.05$), lower wage ceiling ($w = 0.85$), highest mandatory transport/energy share ($\bar{e} = 0.048$)[cite: 1].  
  * **Small Provincial Towns (24.0% Pop / 23.0% Firms):** Moderate housing elasticity ($\delta_H = 0.28, Q = 1.40$), compressed middle-quintile wages ($w = 0.93$), elevated transport costs ($\bar{e} = 0.036$)[cite: 1].  
  * **Large Regional Metros (25.9% Pop / 26.9% Firms — Wellington, Chch, Hamilton, Tauranga, Dunedin):** Constrained urban land ($\delta_H = 0.18, Q = 2.05$), higher knowledge/service wages ($w = 1.05$), moderate transport share ($\bar{e} = 0.022$)[cite: 1].  
  * **Auckland Primate Agglomeration (34.1% Pop / 35.1% Firms — "Paris" Analog):** Severely constrained land supply ($\delta_H = 0.10, Q = 3.10$) and highest trend growth ($G = 1.025$), generating structural land overvaluation, the fattest top-income tail ($w = 1.18, \sigma_z = 0.24$), and severe housing crowd-out for bottom-quintile renters[cite: 1].  
* **Adult Ben-Porath Decay & Positive-Selection Emigration:** Workers entering adulthood stunted ($h_1, h_2$) have a low marginal return to adult training ($I_h^{\alpha_I} h^{\alpha_H}$), causing wages to decay in their 50s[cite: 1]. Meanwhile, high-skill youth ($h_5 \mathrm{--} h_7$) without inherited deposits compare squeezed domestic utility $V_k$ against $V_{\mathrm{Abroad}}$, driving outward emigration between ages 20 and 35[cite: 1].

### **Lens 3: Pensions, The \$1.6T Property Transfer, & Capital Allocation**

* **Weighting Auckland Accurately (34.1% Pop / 35.1% Firms):** Because Auckland house prices ($Q_{\mathrm{Auckland}} = 3.10$) are nearly triple rural levels and 50% above Large regional metros, weighting Auckland at its true Stats NZ share (34.1% rising to ~36.5% by Year 30) increases the national estate/wealth tax base by **8%–10%**, funding both the universal Youth Grant ($E = 0.065$) and expanded ECE ($G_{\mathrm{ECE}} = 0.110$) without raising labor taxes ($\tau_l$)[cite: 1].
* **The Piketty–Guvenen Capital Reallocation Engine:** In an economy where infinite-horizon firms have heterogeneous productivity $z_f \in \lbrace z_\ell, z_h \rbrace$ and face collateral constraints $k \le \lambda_{\mathrm{col}} a_f$, aggregate productivity is $Z = s_h z_h + \left(1 - s_h\right) z_\ell$, where $s_h = \frac{\mu A_h}{\mu A_h + \left(1 - \mu\right) A_\ell}$ is the capital share of high-talent producers[cite: 1]. Taxing book-value wealth/land ($\tau_a$) and passive estates ($\tau_b$) to fund a **Universal Youth Endowment ($E$)** and **First-2000-Days Investment ($G_{\mathrm{ECE}}$)** prunes dormant capital from low-productivity hoarders ($z_\ell$) and reallocates net worth to high-productivity entrepreneurs ($z_h$), raising $s_h$, aggregate productivity $Z$, capital stock $K$, and real wages $w_k$ across the entire economy[cite: 1].

## **Part IV: Methodological Defense & Mathematical Appendix**

This appendix provides the formal mathematical defense of the modeling choices, identification strategies, and computational reductions used to construct the New Zealand Intergenerational Suite[cite: 1].

### **A.1 Defending the Mixed Finite/Infinite-Horizon PType Architecture**

**The Critique:** *Why not model entrepreneurs as finite-horizon life-cycle households, or model all households as infinitely lived dynastic agents?*[cite: 1]  
**The Defense:**

1. In standard Aiyagari life-cycle models, households save in a single risk-free bond/capital pool rented to a static representative firm ($Y = Z K^\alpha L^{1-\alpha}$)[cite: 1]. In that setup, individual portfolio returns are identical ($r$), so a book-value wealth tax $\tau_a$ and a capital income tax $\tau_k$ are mathematically isomorphic ($\tau_a = r \tau_k$), completely missing the **Guvenen et al. (2023) capital misallocation channel**[cite: 1].  
2. Conversely, if heterogeneous entrepreneurial returns $R\left(z_f\right)$ are attached directly to finite-horizon households, retirees at ages 75–95 are forced to operate collateral-constrained firms, confounding life-cycle retirement decumulation (Fan, Seshadri, & Taber; De Nardi, 2004) with corporate equity dynamics[cite: 1].  
3. By partitioning the economy in the VFI Toolkit into `Names_i = {'HH_Rural', 'HH_Small', 'HH_Large', 'HH_Auckland', 'Firm_Rural', 'Firm_Small', 'Firm_Large', 'Firm_Auckland'}` with `N_j.HH_k = 16` and `N_j.Firm_k = Inf`, finite-horizon households supply labor and bond/mutual-fund savings ($a_h$), while infinite-horizon firms face persistent productivity shocks $z_f \in \lbrace z_{\ell,k}, z_{h,k} \rbrace$, borrow up to collateral limit $k \le \lambda_{\mathrm{col}} a_f$, and solve[cite: 1]:  

$$
V_{F,k}\left(a_f, z_f\right) = \max_{a^{\prime}_f \in \lbrack 0, R_k\left(z_f\right) a_f \rbrack} \left\lbrace \ln\left(R_k\left(z_f\right) a_f - a^{\prime}_f\right) + \beta_{\mathrm{firm}} \sum_{z^{\prime}_f} \Pi_{f,k}\left(z^{\prime}_f \vert z_f\right) V_{F,k}\left(a^{\prime}_f, z^{\prime}_f\right) \right\rbrace
$$

where:

$$
R_k\left(z_f\right) = \left(1 - \tau_a\right)^5 + \left(1 - \tau_k\right) \left( r + \max \left\lbrace 0, \alpha_k \left( \frac{1 - \alpha_k}{w_k} \right)^{\frac{1 - \alpha_k}{\alpha_k}} z_f - \left(r + \delta_k\right) \right\rbrace \lambda_{\mathrm{col}} \right)
$$

Because VFI Toolkit loops over `Names_i` sequentially on the GPU, this mixed-horizon synthesis adds **zero state variables** to the household grid while preserving the exact analytical properties of Guvenen et al.[cite: 1].

### **A.2 Defending the Nonparametric Skill Anchoring & Luxury In-Kind Specification**

**The Critique:** *Early childhood test scores have arbitrary scales and are ridden with measurement error; furthermore, standard benefit-incidence studies value public ECE at \$1 per \$1 of spending, which overstates welfare gains for cash-poor families.*[cite: 1]  
**The Defense:**

1. **Adult Outcome Anchoring & Measurement Error Correction:** Following Cunha, Heckman, and Schennach (2010), latent childhood skills $\theta_{k,t}$ are anchored directly in cardinal adult earnings/schooling outcomes $g_{k,j}\left(\theta_{k,t}\right)$ ($h \in H$ at age 20)[cite: 1]. As Cunha et al. demonstrate in Tables I–III, ignoring measurement error in early skill proxies severely attenuates Stage 1 investment loadings and overstates late-stage substitutability; our calibration directly adopts their measurement-error-corrected factor loadings ($\gamma_{1,C,1} = 0.487, \gamma_{2,C,1} = 0.902$)[cite: 1].
2. **Welfare-Based In-Kind Imputation ($\omega_i$):** Following Ferriere et al. (2025), standard monetary imputation values public in-kind spending $G_{\mathrm{ECE}}$ at full cost across all deciles, overstating redistributive compression by up to 50% because constrained families at the corner solution $i_c = 0$ have a high marginal utility of cash $\partial u_i / \partial y_i$[cite: 1]. In `Calibrate_NZ_Moments.m`, we discipline the Stone-Geary luxury parameter $\bar{g}_{\mathrm{ECE}}$ against the empirical share $S = \Pr\left(i_c = 0\right) \approx 0.25$ of fertile households making zero private top-ups, and apply Ferriere et al.'s exact welfare-based imputation weight[cite: 1]:

$$
\omega_i = \frac{\partial u_i / \partial G_{\mathrm{ECE}}}{\partial u_i / \partial y_i} = \min \left\lbrace \frac{y_i / \bar{y}}{F^{-1}\left(S\right)}, 1 \right\rbrace
$$

Even under this strict discounting of direct utility ($\omega_i < 1$ in $Q_1, Q_2$), public $G_{\mathrm{ECE}}$ generates massive positive welfare gains ($\mathrm{CE}\% > 0$) via the concave societal externality $X_t$ and the intergenerational human capital transition matrix $Q_h$[cite: 1].

### **A.3 Defending the Spatial & Housing Dimensionality Reductions**

**The Critique:** *Modeling 4 regions (Rural, Small, Large, Auckland), liquid bonds $b$, physical housing stock $H$, and binary tenure choice (Rent vs. Own) would normally multiply the endogenous state space by $4 \times N_H \times 2$, causing GPU memory overflow.*[cite: 1]  
**The Defense:**  
We achieve exact spatial and housing representation with **zero extra endogenous grid states** via three structural reductions[cite: 1]:

1. **Horizontal Spatial Partitioning (`Names_i`):** Mapping $k \in \lbrace \mathrm{Rural}, \mathrm{Small}, \mathrm{Large}, \mathrm{Auckland} \rbrace$ to VFI Toolkit Permanent Types (`Names_i`) means each region is solved sequentially on the GPU with its own local wage $w_k$, house price $Q_k$, income dispersion $\sigma_z\left(k\right)$, and mandatory fuel/transport subsistence $\bar{e}_k$[cite: 1].  
2. **Net-Worth Consolidation ($a = Q_k h_{\mathrm{house}} - b_{\mathrm{debt}}$):** Following Kiyotaki, Michaelides, and Nikolov (2024), when young homeowners borrow up to the collateral limit, financial debt $b$ and physical housing $h_{\mathrm{house}}$ consolidate into a single state variable: net worth $a \ge 0$[cite: 1]. Intra-period expenditure $e_{\mathrm{tot}}$ splits analytically between non-durable consumption (share $\phi_{\mathrm{nd}}$) and housing space (share $1 - \phi_{\mathrm{nd}}$)[cite: 1].
3. **Closed-Form Tenure Cutoff ($\xi_k$) & Unbalanced Growth Bubble ($G_k$):** Given regional trend growth $G_k$ (Hirano & Toda, 2025), local rental yields $rr_k$ and homeowner user costs $rh_k$[cite: 1]:

$$
rr_k = \left( 1 + \mu_{\mathrm{maint}} - \frac{G_k}{\left(1 + r\right)^{1/5}} \right) Q_k
$$

$$
rh_k = \left( 1 + \mu_{\mathrm{maint}} - \upsilon_h - \frac{G_k}{\left(1 + r\right)^{1/5}} \right) Q_k
$$

pin down the exact fraction of young households choosing leveraged ownership via the uniform preference draw $\chi \sim U\lbrack 0, \bar{\chi} \rbrack$[cite: 1]:

$$
\xi_k = \min \left\lbrace 1, \frac{1}{\bar{\chi}} \left( \frac{rr_k}{rh_k} \right) \right\rbrace
$$

Regional housing markets clear in General Equilibrium against elastic supply $HS\left(k\right) = H_k \left(Q_k\right)^{\delta_{H,k}}$, where $\delta_{H,\mathrm{Auckland}} = 0.10 < \delta_{H,\mathrm{Large}} = 0.18 < \delta_{H,\mathrm{Small}} = 0.28 < \delta_{H,\mathrm{Rural}} = 0.35$[cite: 1].

### **A.4 Defending Warm-Glow + Transition Matrix vs. Explicit Dynastic VFI**

**The Critique:** *Why use a warm-glow bequest function $u_{\mathrm{beq}}\left(a^{\prime}\right)$ and log child-skill payoff $\omega_{ic} \ln\left(h_{\mathrm{child}}\right)$ inside the household Bellman equation rather than solving an outer Barro–Becker Dynastic VFI loop over $V_{\mathrm{child}}$?*[cite: 1]  
**The Defense:**

1. **Distributional Equivalence:** As De Nardi (2004) establishes, embedding a child's full multi-dimensional state vector inside the parent's Bellman equation expands the state space and introduces strategic parent–child gaming, whereas a two-parameter luxury warm-glow function $\left(b_1, b_2\right)$ accurately reproduces the upper tail of the estate distribution and old-age saving profiles[cite: 1].  
2. **Capturing the 75% Intergenerational Multiplier:** In Daruich (2018, Section 6), three-fourths of the long-run welfare gain from early-childhood policy operates through the shift in the **stationary distribution of parent states $\mu\left(a, h, z\right)$** across generations, rather than changes in the curvature of the continuation value function[cite: 1]. Because `Build_IntergenTransitionMatrix.m` maps the exact optimal child investments $i_c^{\ast}\left(a_p, h_p, z_p, k\right)$ and net-of-tax estates $\left(1 - \tau_b\right) a_j^{\prime \ast}\left(k\right)$ into the next generation's initial distribution `jequaloneDist` ($\mu_{1,k}\left(a_1, h_1, z_1\right)$), our architecture captures 100% of this macroeconomic dynastic feedback loop[cite: 1].  
3. **Optional 2-Parameter Dynastic Slope Toggle:** Because a newborn's expected value function $E_{a_1, z_1}\lbrack V_k\left(a_1, h_1, z_1, 1\right) \rbrack$ in a CRRA economy is smooth and concave in initial human capital $h_1$, our log-skill payoff $\omega_{ic} \ln\left(h_{\mathrm{child}}\right)$ is a tight functional proxy for $E\lbrack V_1 \rbrack$[cite: 1]. When evaluating reforms that drastically alter future adult tax progressivity (where farsighted parents might adjust $i_c$ in anticipation of their child's altered after-tax return to skill), one can toggle on a 4-iteration outer loop fitting[cite: 1]:

$$
E_{a_1, z_1}\lbrack V_k\left(a_1, h_1, z_1, 1\right) \rbrack \approx \hat{\alpha}_{0,k} + \hat{\alpha}_{1,k} \frac{h_1^{1 - \eta_{\mathrm{dyn}}}}{1 - \eta_{\mathrm{dyn}}}
$$

(following Lee & Seshadri's $V_4^n \rightarrow V_4^{n+1}$ contraction) with zero change to grid dimensions[cite: 1].
